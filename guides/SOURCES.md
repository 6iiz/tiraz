# Where the guides come from

Every rule in these guides carries one of four marks, so the reader can weigh it:

| Mark | Meaning | How much to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| `[ours: …]` | A lesson or measurement recorded by the kit's authors while making and publishing their own reels and studying reference reels. The key after the colon points into the authors' private production notes (lessons, the brief template, reference notes, the team's notebooks), which are not published. | Tested by the authors, on a small number of reels. Strongest of the four, but not a law. |
| `[adapted: …]` | A rule reworded from a permissively licensed guide. The sources, their authors and licences are in `THIRD-PARTY.md`. | Someone else's craft, not tested by the authors. |
| `[source: …]` | A fact from official platform documentation or a standard, listed with its URL and reading date in `references.md`. | As reliable as its source; platform pages change, so re-read them when it matters. |
| `[judgement]` | Reasoning with no recorded test behind it. | Weakest. Use it when nothing better exists. |

When an adapted rule and one of the authors' tested lessons disagree, the lesson wins, and the guide says so.

The guides hold no brand defaults: colours, fonts, slogans, dialect and banned words always come from the customer's own brand folder (`brand.json`, `voice.md`, `rules.md`).

They were written independently: text was taken only from sources whose licence was confirmed, and those are all listed in `THIRD-PARTY.md`.
