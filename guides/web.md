# Web: a one-page Arabic site in the brand's look

Read this before you design or build a site for a customer. The kit ships no site template: the page is designed from the brand's own look (`<project>/tiraz/brand/look/`), like every reel. Tags: `[ours: …]` (`SOURCES.md`), `[adapted: …]` (`THIRD-PARTY.md`), `[source: …]` (`references.md`), `[judgement]`. Where an adapted rule disagrees with one of ours, ours wins, and the text says so.

## 1. One job

- Name the page's job in one sentence before anything else: what the visitor does when the page works (orders on the store, sends a WhatsApp message, books a slot, visits the shop). Every section either moves the visitor towards that action or is left out. [judgement]
- Know who lands and from where. Most arrive from the brand's own reels, bio link or a shared message, on a phone, already holding the promise the reel made: the page continues that promise in the same voice and look, and never changes it. Ask the customer where their traffic comes from; `audience.md` has who they are. [judgement]
- The action is the working one: the store's product page, a `wa.me` link with the customer's own number, the real booking link. A button that only scrolls to another button does not count. [adapted: impeccable mode-persuade.md]
- A button says what it does. If the customer's action needs something they did not give (a phone number, a store link), the button goes to a channel they did give and is worded for that channel; it never promises one thing and opens another. The missing piece is the first open question for the customer. [ours: product trial 2026-11-17]
- A first-time visitor should know, within seconds and before any scroll, what this is, why it matters to them, and what to do next. [adapted: impeccable new-work.md]
- Order the sections by the visitor's questions, in the order they would ask them: `audience.md` lists their top questions and objections. A question nobody asks gets no section. [judgement]

## 2. True material only

- Every number, price, duration, count and claim comes from `facts.md`, written exactly as it is there. Anything not in that table is not said on the page. [ours: K setup SKILL]
- A fact that lives on a web page (a price, a stock, an offer) is read again from that page on the day you build, and its row in `facts.md` is updated with the date first. [ours: K reel SKILL]
- A condition that changes what the buyer gets (a trial, a limit, an expiry, "link only") stays next to the price it qualifies, at a size the visitor reads without looking for it, and never only in a footnote or a closed question. [ours: K reel SKILL]
- A section with nothing true to put in it is left out, not filled. No invented reviews or ratings, no counters ("+1000 customers") without a `facts.md` row, no "trusted by" or "as seen in" rows, no badges the business does not hold, no stock faces presented as customers. Testimonials are the customer's real clients' words, used with permission, or the section does not exist. [adapted: impeccable new-work.md]
- The source above allows invented demonstration material when it is labelled as such. This kit shows none: a visitor glancing at a phone cannot tell a labelled demonstration from a claim. [judgement]
- A badge or award the business really holds is quoted as its issuer writes it, with its qualifier and year. [judgement]
- Words such as "fastest", "secure", "best", "number one" appear only when `facts.md` has a row that says so and where it comes from. [judgement]
- Contact channels are only the ones the customer gave. Nothing on the page is a placeholder. [judgement]

## 3. The brand's look, on a page

- The look was designed for the brand, not for reels: its ground, colour roles, type, shapes, motion and ending are one system, and the site is that system in another medium. Read `look/look.md` and `look/tokens.json` first; its "always" and "never" lists apply to the page as they apply to a reel. [ours: K ARCHITECTURE]
- A new surface inside an established world keeps the world fixed and decides only its own structure, content, states and interactions. Inventing a second look for the website splits the brand in two. [adapted: impeccable new-work.md]
- `tokens.json` becomes CSS custom properties in one stylesheet, with the same names and roles, and everything else in the stylesheet uses them. What carries over as it is: the colours and their roles, the weights, the ratios between durations. What does not: its sizes are pixels of a 1080x1920 frame, and a reel has no spacing scale. So the page's sizes (in `rem`), its spacing steps and its radii are new decisions: add them to `tokens.json` under a `"web"` key, and translate each named ease once into a CSS `cubic-bezier()`. A shape for that key that has worked: `"web": { "size": { "display": …, "h2": …, "body": … }, "space": [the steps, in rem], "radius": …, "measure": the reading width in ch, "target": the smallest touch target, "ease": { each named ease as a cubic-bezier }, "scene": { the numbers a scene's script reads } }`. [judgement; ours: product trial 2026-11-17, sample pages 2026-11-17]
- What carries over: the meaning of each colour (the accent means on the page what it means in the reels), the type family and the relationships in its scale, the shape grammar taken from the logo (corner radius, stroke weight, angles), the ground's material, the motion personality (its eases and the ratio between its durations). [judgement]
- What must be decided again, because the reel numbers were made for a 1080x1920 frame watched for seconds: the reading width, sizes in `rem` so the visitor's zoom works, density and the pace of the scroll, touch targets, the states of every control, and how the page closes (the reel's ending becomes the page's last section). Write each decision into `look.md` under a "web" heading, with its reason, so the next page reuses it. [judgement]
- The look carries the page: its material is the ground and the major surfaces, and its type sets the headlines. A look reduced to an accent colour on a neutral layout is the category's page with a sticker on it. [adapted: impeccable mode-persuade.md]
- The common skeleton stays a template whatever colours it wears: a centred headline over a sub-line and a button, then a row of equal cards, a stats band, a logo strip, a footer. If the brand's colours could be swapped for a competitor's without the page noticing, the layout was not designed. [adapted: impeccable mode-persuade.md]
- Run the two tests of `look.md` section 2 on the first screen: with the logo covered, is it still theirs? With a competitor's logo, would it still work? (It should not.) [judgement]

## 4. The first screen

- Before any scroll, at 390x844, the visitor sees: what this is (the headline, in the brand's voice), why it matters to them, the action, and one thing only this brand can show: the real product, the real place, a real fact. [adapted: impeccable mode-persuade.md]
- The memory test: if someone left after one screen, what would they describe an hour later? If the honest answer is a mood, the first screen is not done. [adapted: impeccable new-work.md]
- The headline is the page's only `h1`. [ours: K check.mjs]
- Text over a picture keeps at least 4.5:1 against the brightest and the darkest part of what is behind it, at every size the picture is cropped to. How it does so (a backing shape, a quieter part of the picture, a scrim, moving the text) is a decision of the look, made for this brand. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 contrast minimum]
- `object-fit: cover` crops the same picture differently on a phone and on a desktop. Check that the subject and the text's calm area survive both; give the phone its own crop with `<picture>` when they do not. [adapted: impeccable adapt.md]
- Mark the element that holds text over a picture, video, canvas, gradient or texture with `data-over-picture` (on the text's container, not on the picture). `web/check.mjs` then measures that text against the pixels behind it; unmarked text over a picture is reported as a problem. Use the mark too when the ground behind a text is painted by something the colour check cannot see: a pseudo-element, a layer placed behind it, a texture. Do not put it on a plain coloured surface: the colour check already measures that, and the pixel measure counts everything behind the text's box, a border or a line that crosses it included (which is then a real fault: the text is sitting on its own outline). [ours: K check.mjs, product trial 2026-11-17]
- Pictures are the customer's own files. A stock or generated picture is never presented as their shop, product or client. [judgement]

## 5. Arabic and right to left on the web

- Put both on the root element, `<html lang="ar" dir="rtl">`: language does not imply direction, and direction belongs in the markup, not only in CSS. [source: W3C Internationalization, qa-html-dir]
- Lay out with logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `padding-block`, `inset-inline-end`, `text-align: start`), so the page is right to left by construction. [source: W3C Internationalization, qa-html-dir]
- Digits run left to right inside Arabic, and neutral characters (spaces, `+`, `%`, `!`, brackets) take the base direction between runs, so a `+` or `%` beside a number can land on the wrong side. Read every number with a sign, a range, a currency or a percentage on both screenshots; where one lands wrong, isolate the run (`<bdi>` or an inline `dir`). [source: W3C Internationalization, UBA basics]
- A URL, an email, a handle, a code or a phone number is an inline left-to-right island: `<span dir="ltr">`, never `dir` on a block (it also flips the block's alignment). A phone number with its country code is one island. [adapted: designing-arabic-frontends]
- One digit system on the whole page, including buttons, labels and alt text; `arabic-text.md` section 3 decides which. Currency follows the amount. [adapted: designing-arabic-frontends]
- Do not make a line of Arabic text a flex container: each text run and inline element becomes its own item and the line's order breaks. A number inside a flex `<summary>` split a question into three pieces; the script passed, the screenshot showed it. [ours: web NOTES]
- Fonts are the customer's own files (or the kit font they chose), copied into the site folder and declared with `@font-face`; no font service. Load only the weights the page uses. [adapted: impeccable typeset.md]
- Serve the font as WOFF2. `python <kit>/tools/assets.py webfont <font file> --out <site folder>/fonts` writes the same font again in that packing, with every glyph, every weight of a variable font and all the shaping rules kept, so Arabic joins exactly as before; name it in `@font-face` with `format("woff2")` and keep the licence file beside it. Across the kit's own 49 font files it came out 51% to 82% smaller, 65% at the middle. It never takes letters out to save more: a sentence the customer changes next month must still find its letters. The command needs `fonttools` and `brotli`; without them the `.ttf` works as it is, and the check says so in a note. [ours: K tools/assets.py, measured 2026-11-18]
- Buttons and form fields do not inherit the page's font in browsers: give them `font: inherit`. In our test page a plain `<button>` showed in Arial and `check.mjs` reported it. [ours: K check.mjs]
- Line height about 1.6 or more for Arabic (marks and descenders clip under tighter values). A display line of two or three words may be set tighter, down to about 1.25, only after the capture shows that no mark or tail touches the line beside it. [adapted: designing-arabic-frontends; judgement]
- No letter-spacing on anything that contains Arabic, mixed spans such as «250 كم» included; no italics or fake slant. Weights 400 to 700 at reading sizes. [adapted: designing-arabic-frontends]
- Mirror icons by meaning: forward and back arrows and chevrons flip in RTL (`transform: scaleX(-1)`); clocks, search, ticks, play buttons, logos and anything with Latin letters or digits do not. [adapted: designing-arabic-frontends]

## 6. Craft that separates a designed page from a generated one

- Squint at the screenshot: with the detail blurred, the primary element, the secondary one and the groups should still read in order. [adapted: impeccable layout.md]
- Group by proximity before adding containers. Make rhythm from a deliberate contrast between tight and generous intervals taken from one spacing scale in `tokens.json`, with more space above a heading than below it. [adapted: impeccable layout.md]
- Prose stays within a comfortable measure; the source gives 45 to 75 characters for Latin text. Set the reading column in `ch` or `rem` and read it in Arabic on both screenshots, since no measured figure for Arabic is recorded here. [adapted: impeccable typeset.md]
- Body text is at least 1rem. Use few type roles with unmistakable steps between them, made from size, weight and space together, not size alone. [adapted: impeccable typeset.md]
- Headings wrap with `text-wrap: balance`, body text with `text-wrap: pretty`; check every heading's break at every width it renders at. [adapted: impeccable typeset.md]
- The accent keeps the meaning the look gave it and is spent on the action and the one thing that matters. A strong colour that owns one region does more than small accents scattered everywhere. [adapted: impeccable colorize.md]
- On a coloured surface, derive secondary text from that surface's hue or the foreground, not a generic grey. [adapted: impeccable craft-floor.md]
- The parts nobody draws still ship: text selection, the focus ring, the caret, the scrollbar, link underline offset. Give them the brand's palette. [adapted: impeccable craft-floor.md]
- Defaults to notice. None is banned; each is fine when chosen for a reason about this brand, and not fine when arrived at by habit. [adapted: impeccable craft-floor.md]

| The default | Ask |
|---|---|
| Rows of same-size cards, each an icon, a heading and a line | Are these things truly equal, or is it the framework's habit? |
| A big number with a small label, repeated in a band | Is each number a `facts.md` row that the visitor needs? |
| A small label above every heading, or section numbers 01 / 02 / 03 | Does the label or the sequence tell the visitor anything? |
| Gradient text, glass and blur panels, a thick coloured side border on cards | Did this come from the look, or from habit? |
| Emoji or unicode symbols standing in for icons | Are the icons drawn, in one stroke and weight? |
| A system font as the display face | Is the brand's font loaded? |

## 7. States and resilience

- Every link and button has a hover state (inside `@media (hover: hover)`), a clearly visible `:focus-visible` state and an active state. Nothing that matters is reachable only by hover. [adapted: impeccable adapt.md]
- Touch targets are at least 44x44 CSS pixels on a phone, with space between neighbours, even when the visible mark is smaller. [adapted: impeccable adapt.md]
- Try the longest and the shortest real content: the longest product name, a two-line headline, a one-line answer. No fixed widths on text containers; flex and grid children that hold text get `min-width: 0`. [adapted: impeccable harden.md]
- A missing image leaves its alt text and a ground behind it, not a broken layout: give every image its `width` and `height` and a background colour on its box. [judgement]
- Look at a narrow phone (320 CSS pixels wide) and a wide desktop (1920): the checker opens only 390 and 1440. A wide screen needs a maximum width for reading lines; a narrow one must not scroll sideways. [adapted: impeccable adapt.md]
- Text zoomed to 200% loses no content. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 resize text]
- Containers survive a visitor's own larger line, word and paragraph spacing without clipping. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 text spacing]
- If anything touches the screen's edges, use `viewport-fit=cover` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding. [adapted: impeccable adapt.md]
- With JavaScript off, every word of the page is present and visible; scripts only add. [ours: K check.mjs]
- Decorative layers (a paper grain, a soft light, a big shape behind a section) stay inside the page's width: size them in percentages of their section, or clip their wrapper. A wide layer painted by `::before` or `::after` makes the page scroll sideways, and the check cannot name it because it is not an element. A layer with `position: fixed` shows a seam in the full-page capture that a visitor never sees: judge it on the top capture. [ours: product trial 2026-11-17]
- Text inside a rounded shape (a pill button, an arch, a circle) keeps clear of the curve: the ends of a line reach further than they look, and a word that touches an outline of its own colour merges with it. The check measures this. [ours: product trial 2026-11-17]

## 8. Motion on a page

- The look's motion personality carries over; its job changes. On a page, motion acknowledges an action, explains a change of state, or gives one authored moment. The same fade-and-rise on every section is not a decision. [adapted: impeccable animate.md]
- A reference for durations: about 100 to 150 ms for feedback, 150 to 300 ms for a change of state, 300 to 500 ms for layout, 500 to 800 ms for one deliberate entrance; exits faster than entrances. The look's eases apply; its durations were made for reels and may need shortening for feedback. [adapted: impeccable animate.md]
- Content is visible in its default state; a reveal is an enhancement, never CSS that hides text until a script runs. Animate `transform` and `opacity`, not `width`, `height` or margins. [adapted: impeccable animate.md]
- Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` path that removes movement and keeps changes of colour and opacity that carry meaning. [source: MDN prefers-reduced-motion]
- Under reduced motion, `check.mjs` fails any animation still running 1.2 s after load (a decorative loop included), any text or picture still hidden, and any canvas that is still changing (section 11). [ours: K check.mjs]
- Nothing flashes more than three times in one second. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 three flashes]
- A loop that is not essential stops when it is off screen or the tab is hidden. [adapted: impeccable animate.md]
- Nothing moves behind a line while it is being read. [judgement]
- The scroll belongs to the visitor: no hijacked scrolling, no section that holds the page until an animation ends. A tall section that keeps its screen in place while the visitor scrolls through it (section 12) is not that: the scroll keeps its own speed, and scrolling on always leaves. [judgement]

## 9. Accessibility and performance facts

- Text contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text, computed ratios never rounded up. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 contrast minimum]
- The kit's checker holds text to those two levels: 3:1 for large text (24 px and up, or 18.66 px and up in bold), 4.5:1 for everything else. An accent colour that is under 4.5:1 on the ground can therefore carry a large headline word and nothing smaller; for small things it becomes a mark (an underline, a ring, a dot) instead of text. [ours: K check.mjs, product trial 2026-11-17]
- Controls and the graphics a visitor needs to understand the page: 3:1 against the colours next to them. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 non-text contrast]
- A focus indicator is held to the same 3:1. [adapted: impeccable colorize.md]
- Every image has an `alt`: what the picture shows that matters for the job, or `alt=""` when it is decoration. An image that is the whole content of a link names where the link goes. [judgement]
- One `h1`, then an `h2` per section in order, no skipped levels; the content in one `<main>`. [judgement]
- Keyboard order follows the visual order (right to left, top to bottom). [adapted: impeccable layout.md]
- A page that opens with a bar of links, or keeps one on screen, starts with a "skip to the content" link that shows when it takes the keyboard focus. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 bypass blocks]
- The head says what the page is: a `<title>`, a `<meta name="description">` of one true sentence in the brand's voice, and for shared links `og:title`, `og:description` and `og:image` (a 1200x630 picture made from the page's own first screen or scene, kept in the site folder). An `og:image` that every service reads, a canonical link, `robots.txt` and a sitemap all need the page's final address: when the customer has not given it, write what can be written, leave the rest out, and put the address first among the open questions. The check notes what is missing. [judgement; ours: K check.mjs]
- A video with speech has captions. [source: W3C WCAG 2.2 captions prerecorded]
- No request goes to another host: no font service, no CDN script, no analytics, no embedded map or video from another site. [ours: K check.mjs]
- Pictures are exported near the size they display at (about twice that for sharp screens) and carry `width` and `height` so nothing jumps while the page loads. [judgement]
- A good largest contentful paint is 2.5 s or less and a good cumulative layout shift 0.1 or less, judged at the 75th percentile of page loads, phone and desktop separately. [source: web.dev LCP, CLS, vitals]
- A little JavaScript only where it earns its place; the page's text never depends on it. [judgement]

## 10. What `web/check.mjs` measures, and what it cannot tell you

Run `node <kit>/web/check.mjs <site folder> [--out <folder>] [--draft] [--more] [--shots <n>]`. `--draft` is for a page that is not finished (a first screen alone): the rule about too little text is left out. `--more` also opens the page 320 and 1920 px wide, with the text size doubled, with keyboard focus on its first three controls and with the pointer over its first action. Then it walks down the page on a desktop and on a phone the way a hand scrolls, in steps of a tenth of the window, and pictures the window screen by screen, 1.1 s after each stop (`more-scroll-desktop-NN.png`, `more-scroll-phone-NN.png`, up to 16 each, or `--shots <n>` for a long page or short moments: the only pictures that show a scene fixed behind the page or a moment tied to the scroll), then walks it 320 px wide and under reduced motion on a desktop, four places each (`more-320-N.png`, `more-reduced-N.png`). At every one of those places, and half way between two pictures of the walk, each piece of text in the window is measured against the pixels really behind it there (below). It writes `more-*.png` to look at, and reports sideways scrolling with the things that cause it (widest first), invisible focus, and any control that is in the window but lies under something else on arrival or at the end of the page, where no scrolling can bring it out (in one build a sticky band covered the closing button on a phone, and every other measure passed). A last screen that is bare for more than 40% of its height is a note, and so is a font served as `.ttf` or `.otf` (section 5). It serves the folder on 127.0.0.1, opens it in Chrome through the kit's Playwright (`TIRAZ_CHROMIUM` first, then the installed Chrome, then Playwright's own), and writes `390x844-top.png`, `390x844-full.png`, `1440x900-top.png`, `1440x900-full.png`, `390x844-nojs-full.png` and `check.json` to `<site folder>.check/`. Exit code 1 means at least one problem; 2 means the folder has no `index.html`. [ours: K check.mjs]

The markup it relies on, none of which says how the page looks: `lang` and `dir` on `<html>`; exactly one `h1`; the content inside one `<main>`; every font served from the folder through `@font-face`; `data-over-picture` on each element that holds text over a picture, video, canvas, gradient or texture. [ours: K check.mjs]

At 390x844 (as a touch phone) and at 1440x900, once the fonts are ready and 1.8 s more have passed, it takes the top screenshot, scrolls through the page so scroll effects fire, and reports: [ours: K check.mjs]
- a missing `lang` or `dir` on `<html>`, or a count of `h1` other than one;
- sideways scrolling, and any element that reaches past the page's own edge and is not fixed and not inside a clipping parent, the widest reach first (measured from the page's edge, so a right-to-left page that already stands scrolled still names the true cause);
- an `img` without `alt`, an image that is a link's only content with empty alt, an image that did not load;
- `#` links to ids that do not exist, local links that return an error; links to other sites are listed, not opened;
- console errors, failed requests, error statuses, and any request to a host other than the local one (the check refuses such a request before it is sent, so it never contacts another machine, and what the request would have loaded is missing from the screenshots);
- every font family that visible text asks for first, which must be a face loaded from the site (a generic or system family, or a face that failed, is a problem);
- the contrast of body text on the page ground, and of every visible text against the background colours composited behind it: under 3:1 for large text, under 4.5:1 for the rest;
- text that touches or crosses the rounded edge of the shape that holds it (a pill, an arch, a circle), decided from the pixels of the text itself;
- text that sits on a background image or gradient, or whose centre lies over an `img`, `video`, `canvas`, `picture`, `svg`, `iframe`, `object` or `embed` of 48 px or more, without a `data-over-picture` container. A picture or canvas that fills most of the window (three fifths or more) and stays there while the page scrolls (`position: fixed` or `sticky`) counts as lying under every text that passes over it, not only the text that is over it at the top of the page. A band that holds part of the window, with the words in the rest, is not counted this way: whether its words ever stand over it is left to the pixels of the walk. Text that stands on a solid colour of its own, which the picture is not part of, is not counted;
- text inside `data-over-picture`: the page is walked to it, it is brought to the middle of the window, and its place is read there, so text on a screen that stays is measured where it stands once the screen has stuck. The part of a line that lies under something that stays in the window and has a solid ground of its own (a bar, a band) is left out: the browser is asked what is piled over the line, and the line is cut back to what shows, clear of the covering edge. A text with no line left there is brought lower, then higher, until it shows. Then the window is pictured with all text made transparent (text shadow removed) and again with all text painted in one loud colour: a line where the two pictures do not differ holds no letters on screen and is left out too. Each text element is measured against the brightest and the darkest 1% of the pixels behind its lines, at the same two levels.

On the walk (`--more`) the same measure is taken of every piece of text in the window, marked or not, at each picture, half way between two pictures, and at the four places 320 px wide. It finds what one place per text cannot: words that a moving thing passes behind further down the page, a mark under the rim of a travelling object, a price under falling beans. Each piece is reported once, where it was hardest to read, with how far down the page that was and the picture that shows it (`more-behind-<desktop|phone>-NN.png` when the place is not one of the walk's own pictures). Text that is faded at that moment (arriving or leaving, its own opacity times its parents' under 0.95), lines that do not show (under a bar or a band, cut off by a clipping parent: words sliding under a bar is how a page scrolls), and text whose colour pixels cannot give (an emoji, a blend mode, a picture painted inside the letters, text inside an `svg`) are left out. So dim a text with a colour, never with opacity, or it is not measured here. In the build that led to this measure, the builder's own count found six such faults on a page the check had passed, among them hour marks at 1.03:1 under a travelling cup and a price at 2.83:1 under passing beans. On the other sample page the new measure named one text, a dimension's label lying on its own line at 2.08:1 on a 320 px screen: the fault a blind reader had found by eye on the phone pictures. [ours: K check.mjs, web/test_check.mjs; sample pages 2026-11-18]

With JavaScript off (390x844): no `<main>`, a missing headline, any `h1`, `h2`, `h3`, `p` or `summary` in `<main>` that is hidden, of zero size or faded (its own opacity times its parents' under 0.99; answers inside a closed `<details>` are skipped), a picture left invisible, fewer than 200 characters of text. With reduced motion: the same hidden-text and picture checks, any running animation, console errors, and each canvas of 48 px or more (the first four) pictured twice, 0.7 s apart: one that changed is a problem. Notes list external links, module scripts that will not run from a double-clicked file, a page with no `<meta name="description">`, and missing `og:title`, `og:description` or `og:image` (what a shared link shows). [ours: K check.mjs]

Three things about text and what lies behind it, each learned from a page that passed late: [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- A pattern on the ground (a grid, a grain) painted as the `background-image` of the page or of a section makes every text in it "text over a gradient". Paint the pattern on its own layer behind the content and mark the element that holds the words: a faint pattern passes the measure easily. Do not lay a plain patch under each block of words to escape the measure: it cuts a visible hole in the pattern, and a juror who knew nothing of the reason read those patches as accidents.
- Words that scroll past a scene that stays put (a fixed scene, a sticky band) are measured with the scene behind them, where they are read and again at every place the walk stops. If the scene can pass behind the words, give the words a ground that belongs to the look (a sheet, a label, a block with its own edge), never a bare rectangle.
- Words that pass under a bar or a band are simply not judged while they are under it; the check asks about covered controls only on arrival and at the end, so look at the scroll pictures for words cut by a bar on the way.

What it cannot tell you: whether the page looks right, or looks like this brand; whether a font that loaded has the Arabic letters (a Latin-only face falls back letter by letter); hover, focus and active states beyond what `--more` pictures (the first three controls, the first action); a control covered somewhere in the middle of the page; a scene drawn on a canvas beyond that one test (whether it runs smoothly, whether it stops off screen, what it costs a phone); what a scene that moves by itself puts behind a text between the moments the walk stops at; text between two of those places on a page whose moments are shorter than half a window (give `--shots` a larger number); pictures painted by pseudo-elements; touch target sizes, heading order, alt text quality, reading width; browser zoom (`--more` doubles the text size only); any width other than 390 and 1440 (and 320 and 1920 with `--more`), any browser other than Chromium, any real phone; load speed; and whether a single number on the page is in `facts.md`, which it never reads. [ours: K check.mjs] Scripts pass pages that are visibly wrong: a picture rendered as a small box and a question split in three both passed the script and were caught on the screenshots. Open them. [ours: web NOTES]

## 11. A scene drawn by script (a canvas: three.js, or a 2D drawing)

A page may carry a scene drawn by script: an object in three dimensions, a field of particles, a line drawing that assembles, a drawing that follows the pointer. It is built from the brand's own shapes and material, it serves one of the page's moments (section 12), and it is never the content. Decide it in the plan, for a reason about this brand.

**The mechanics are in the kit.** Copy `<kit>/web/kit/stage.js` to `site/kit/stage.js`. It mounts two kinds of scene with the same care: `createStage(THREE, { box, still, rest })` for three.js (copy `<kit>/vendor/three/`, the library and its `LICENSE`, to `site/vendor/three/`), and `createDrawing({ box, still, rest })` for a scene drawn on a 2D canvas, which needs no library. Both take care of what every scene owes the visitor: the still until the first frame is drawn, one frame and no loop under reduced motion, no drawing off screen or in a hidden tab, a pixel ratio capped at 2, the size of its box, and the still back when the browser refuses. `still` is one element or a list of them, for a scene that carries several moments. When the browser refuses the scene or takes it away, the box and `<html>` get the class `no-scene`. `studio()` makes a reflection map from a few lit panels, and `softShadow()` a round shadow that fades at its edge (`scale.x` is its width, `scale.z` its depth). The page's own scene file then holds only the scene. The file's first lines show how each is called, when a frame is drawn, and what `web/still.mjs` asks of a scene. [ours: K web/kit/stage.js, web/test_kit.mjs]

- Which kind. Lit objects, materials and depth of field are three.js. Thin lines, flat shapes, a diagram, a field of marks are a 2D drawing: lighter (no library to load), exact at hairline weights, and the same code draws the still. One sample page drew a machine of 26 boxes with hidden lines removed on a 2D canvas, from its own small projection, in about 10 KB of script. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]

- three.js here is the core library alone, release 170: 692 KB, 171 KB compressed. Loaders, controls, an environment file and post-processing are not included, and nothing is fetched from another host. Build shapes from the core's own geometries (a lathe for anything turned, a tube along a curve, instances for many small things), reflections from `studio()`, and surface texture from a 2D canvas drawn by script (speckle, grain, a printed label). [ours: K vendor/three, sample page 2026-11-17]
- The words stay in HTML. The headline, the price and the action are text above or beside the canvas, never pixels inside it: a canvas is not read aloud, cannot be selected or searched, and is empty with JavaScript off. [judgement]
- Arabic is never built from letter outlines inside the scene: a text shape made one letter after another is not joined and runs the wrong way. A word that must live in the scene is drawn by the browser on a 2D canvas (`fillText`, which shapes Arabic) and used as a texture. [judgement]
- The still. The scene's box has its size in CSS and holds the still (an `img`) before any script runs, so the place is never empty and nothing jumps. Make the still from the scene itself: `node <kit>/web/still.mjs <site folder> <site folder>/img/scene.webp` pictures one frame at the scene's resting moment (`rest`, or `--t <number>`), with its transparency. `stage.js` takes the still away once the first frame is drawn. Do not fade it out with opacity (a picture under 0.1 opacity counts as "waiting to appear" in the check), and do not leave both showing: a see-through canvas over its still shows the object twice. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- A scene that follows the scroll has no clock: its moments are places in the story. Its code reads its place from the scroll as usual, and from `stage.rest` when `stage.asStill` is true, so `still.mjs --t 3` pictures the third beat (or `--t 0.6`, if the scene counts from 0 to 1). A page then keeps one still per moment, each standing where that moment's words are when JavaScript is off. When the page lays words or numbers over the scene as HTML and the still should carry them, add `--screen`: it pictures the scene's box as the page shows it, words and ground included. A scene the size of the window composes itself for whatever window it is given, so picture each moment in a window the shape its still will have (`--width`, `--height`). [ours: K web/still.mjs, sample pages 2026-11-17]
- Stills that are not in the scene's own box (one per moment, each beside its words) are ordinary `img` elements in the markup, so they are there when nothing runs. One rule of the stylesheet decides when they give way:

  ```css
  html.js:not(.no-scene) .moment-still { display: none; }   /* the script is running and the scene was not refused: it draws these moments itself */
  ```

  With JavaScript off the class `js` is never there; when the script never arrives the line in the page's head takes it away (section 12); when the browser refuses the scene or takes it away, `stage.js` adds `no-scene`. All three times the stills show. Do not put a still in a `<noscript>`: a browser with JavaScript on never renders it, even when the script itself failed, so nothing can bring it back. [ours: sample pages 2026-11-17, K web/kit/stage.js]
- An opening that draws itself from nothing is the one case where the still must not show while the script is on its way: the finished drawing would flash before the pen starts. Hide that still under `html.js` as above and keep the box's size in CSS; until the scene mounts the first screen then shows its words on the ground, which is what a visitor on a slow connection sees for a moment. [ours: sample pages 2026-11-17]
- Numbers the scene's script needs (distances, counts, the beats of the story) are decisions of the look like any other: they live in `tokens.json` under `web`, and reach the script as custom properties read with `getComputedStyle`, never written a second time in the script. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- A see-through canvas has edges. A shadow cast on an invisible floor is cut where the canvas ends, a hard line that shows on wide screens: use `softShadow()`, or let the scene fill the screen, and look at the 1920 capture. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- Glossy, glazed and metal materials need something to reflect or they render dark and flat: give the scene `studio()` panels, placed and coloured as the look's light. The reflection map is small, so a crisp highlight (a lamp on a liquid, a window on glass) is drawn as its own shape. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- Light changes a colour: a brand colour on a lit, tone-mapped material is not that colour on screen (in one build a cream glaze came out pink-beige and a coffee brown came out red). Choose the scene's colours by looking at the capture, record them in `tokens.json` beside the brand colours they stand for, and keep exact brand colours for unlit things. Read the brand's colours from the page's custom properties (`getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).getPropertyValue('--accent')`), written as hex, `rgb()` or `hsl()`. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- Reduced motion: `stage.js` draws the one frame. A change the visitor makes (scrolling to a new state, choosing something) may still redraw it: call `stage.invalidate()`. `check.mjs` pictures each canvas twice under reduced motion and fails one that changed by itself. [ours: K check.mjs]
- A scene that only answers the scroll or the hand draws when asked, not on every frame: its function returns `false` once it has settled (or the stage is created with `continuous: false`), and the page calls `stage.invalidate()` when the scroll or the hand moves. While something is still easing toward its place the function returns `true`. For a slow drift that needs no more than a few pictures a second, it returns a number: the seconds to wait before the next frame (`return 0.05` draws twenty times a second, and the canvas keeps its picture in between). [ours: K web/kit/stage.js, web/test_kit.mjs]
- The scroll stays the visitor's: the canvas takes no wheel and no drag that would hold the page (`stage.js` sets `touch-action: pan-y`), and no camera control zooms on scroll. [judgement]
- Text whose centre lies over the canvas needs `data-over-picture` (section 4). When the scene stays in the window while the page scrolls, that is every text that passes over it: mark the element that holds the page's words (`main`, or each section). Marked text is measured where it is read, and with `--more` every text is measured again at each place the walk stops (section 10). A glow or gradient painted on a whole section makes every text in it "text over a gradient" for the check: paint it on the scene's own box. [ours: K check.mjs, sample pages 2026-11-17]
- The measure is against the darkest and the brightest 1% of what is behind the text, so one thin line of a drawing passing behind a word of the same colour fails it, and rightly: the word and the line merge. Overlap needs a difference of tone (light type over a dark object, or the reverse). Where the scene and the type share a colour, as in an ink drawing with ink type, they interlock instead: the drawing passes between the lines of type and round them, never through them. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- For assistive technology the canvas is decoration, unless it shows something the visitor needs: then give the stage a `label` that says what it shows. [judgement]
- What the check cannot tell you about a scene: whether it looks right, whether it runs smoothly on a real phone, and what it costs the battery. It runs in Chrome on this machine only. That the scene stops off screen and that the still stays without WebGL is `stage.js`'s work, proved by the kit's own test, not by the page check. [ours: K check.mjs, web/test_kit.mjs]

## 12. A page people remember

Sections 1 to 11 make a page correct. A correct page can still be ordinary: a headline, a picture and a button, then sections of equal weight. The pages visitors send to each other, and that juries of designers reward, are made differently. Awwwards scores a site on design (40%), usability (30%), creativity (20%) and content (10%) [source: Awwwards evaluation]. In the pages studied for this section, what earned those points was an idea that could not be moved to another brand, carried through every screen, and craft in what happens between one state and the next. [ours: site references 2026-11]

### Plan it as moments

- Before any code, write the page as a short film the visitor plays by scrolling: an opening, three to five moments, a closing. For each section: what the visitor sees happen, which of the brand's own things does it, what the visitor's hand does (scrolls, points, drags, chooses), the one line of words it carries, and the still that stands in when nothing may move. This storyboard goes into `plan.md` and is agreed with the page (the site skill, step 1). [ours: site references 2026-11]
- Each moment comes from the brand: what marks them when they are seen (`answers.md`), their product, the gesture of their trade (pouring, stamping, assembling, measuring). A moment that would suit a competitor as well is decoration: cut it. Do not take a moment from another brand's page, however good; take the way it was made. [ours: site references 2026-11]
- One world. The page has one ground, one material and one light from the first screen to the last. Sections change its state (its colour, its light, how its objects are arranged), never its identity. [ours: site references 2026-11]
- Vary the rhythm on purpose: a screen that is one moment, then a quiet dense block of facts, then a moment. Sections of the same height and build, one after another, read as a template (section 3). The commonest case is a scene held on one side with a small column of words passing on the other, for every moment: a juror who saw only the pictures marked one page down for "four sections in a row" built that way. After the first screen the type must still take part in the picture: compose at least some moments with the words at a scale that belongs to the scene. [judgement; ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- A mark the visitor has to decode is explained where it appears: a number on a part has its name, an icon its word. A page whose own maker knows what "1 to 6" are is not yet a page for someone who does not. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- The facts still lead. A moment carries a row of `facts.md` or sets up the action; one that carries nothing is cut, however good it looks. [judgement]

### The first screen is a poster

- The scene or picture is the ground of the whole first screen, edge to edge, and the headline sits in its calm area. A scene in a box beside a column of text is a product shot on a page; the same scene filling the screen is a world. [ours: site references 2026-11, sample pages 2026-11-17]
- Type is part of the picture: the headline or the brand's word is set large enough to be a shape (in the pages studied, a word as wide as the screen), and the object crosses it, in front or behind. Depth comes from overlap, and overlap needs a difference of tone: light type over a dark object or the reverse, or the contrast measure fails the words where the two meet, and rightly (section 11). Where the object and the type share a colour, the object passes between the lines and round them. In Arabic this means whole words at display size, never stretched or spaced letters (`arabic-text.md`). [ours: site references 2026-11, sample pages 2026-11-17]
- Small exact things at the edges make a scene read as real: the hours, a date, a measure with its unit, set small in a plain technical face. They are rows of `facts.md` placed with care, never invented texture. [ours: site references 2026-11]
- Capture the first screen and look at it alone, as a poster: does the still frame hold without motion? Then run the two tests of `look.md` section 2 on it. [judgement]

### Moments, and how each is made

| The moment | How it is made | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| An opening that becomes the page: the logo or the product draws itself, fills, opens | a short timeline, under two seconds, that ends as the first screen; the page under it is already complete | never a wait for loading alone; left out under reduced motion; the action is never blocked |
| An object that answers the hand | the pointer's place, eased, turns or tilts it a few degrees (`pointer()`, `damp()`); on touch it follows a sideways drag while the finger is down (`pointer().down`, `.type`; how far the drag has gone is `x - startX`) and returns when it lifts | small angles; it rests when the hand leaves; the page still scrolls up and down under the finger |
| Cards that lean toward the pointer | `lean()` gives two angles as custom properties; the stylesheet turns the card with `perspective()` | only where a pointer hovers |
| A world that changes with the section | `sectionState()` writes the section crossing the middle of the window (or another `line` of it) to `<html data-state>`; the colours are custom properties under each state, with a `transition` on the ones that change | the contrast in every state: the check measures the first |
| Depth from flat layers | cut-out shapes moving at 0.2 to 0.6 of the scroll (`depth()`): the small ones slower and slightly blurred, the large ones faster and sharp | `transform` only; a handful of layers |
| An object that travels through the page | one scene fixed behind the page; `progress()` of each section moves, turns and lights it | the words keep their own ground |
| A scene the scroll plays | a tall section with a `position: sticky` screen inside; `pinned()` gives 0 to 1 across it and drives the scene (with `{ top, bottom }` when a band or a bar already covers part of the window, as on a phone) | the scroll is never slowed, snapped or held: scrolling on always leaves; what sticks lets go before the closing |
| A number that counts to its fact | `count()`, set up when the page mounts: once, when seen, ending on the text exactly as written. A number already in front of the visitor at that moment stays as written (counting it would show it, take it to zero and bring it back) | the final value is in the HTML |
| A drawing that draws itself | each path's `stroke-dashoffset`, animated with the Web Animations API when it is seen | drawn in the default state (a CSS transition on it did not run in one build, and the check passed the blank drawing) |
| A closing that pays off | the last screen returns to the opening's object, completes its gesture and ends on the action | one action, the page's own |

`<kit>/web/kit/scroll.js` holds these mechanics: copy it to `site/kit/scroll.js`. It has no look: the distances, angles, durations and eases are the brand's, written in the page's stylesheet. [ours: K web/kit/scroll.js, web/test_kit.mjs; site references 2026-11; sample page 2026-11-17]

### Between states

- A control answers in the page's own motion, not with a change of colour alone: the brand's shape closes round the button, the arrow travels, the card leans. [judgement]
- Text arrives by whole lines, each rising inside its own clipped box, in the order it is read, or by whole words when the brand's `look.md` says so: the look decides. Arabic is never animated letter by letter (`arabic-text.md`). [ours: K arabic-text]
- Nothing teleports: a colour melts into the next, an object carries the eye from one section into the next. A hard cut between two states is where a page looks unfinished. [ours: site references 2026-11]
- When the hand is still, one thing still breathes (a light that turns slowly, a surface that settles), unless reduced motion is asked. A page that freezes the moment the visitor stops reads as a picture of a page. [judgement]
- One family of eases and one set of durations, from the look (section 8). [judgement]

### What stays on screen, and the end

- Anything that stays while the page scrolls (a bar with the action, a band that holds the scene on a phone, the logo) is designed in its stuck state too: what it covers, where it sits in relation to the page's frame, how the visitor gets back to the top, and how it answers the pointer. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- What sticks lets go before the closing. The last screen of the page is the closing whole: its object, its line and its action on screen together, nothing lying over them, and nothing after them. Scroll to the very end in small steps and look: in one build the closing block slid under a sticky band on a phone, the last screen was half bare, and every measure had passed. `--more` now reports a control left under something at the end and notes a bare last screen; the picture is still yours to open. [ours: sample page 2026-11-17, K check.mjs]
- A film can be stopped anywhere. Look at the pictures that fall between two moments as well: a sheet halfway out of its slot, a part halfway along its path, must read as what it is, not as a stray shape. A blind reader marked one page down for exactly this, twice: a card that slid over the drawing with parts poking out from under it, and a panel drawn as a second outline on top of the first. Marks that belong to the resting state (numbers on parts, labels) are put away while a thing travels. [ours: sample pages 2026-11-17]
- A screen that stays is held by its parent element, not by the grid area it is placed in: a `position: sticky` grid item keeps sticking until the whole grid has passed (measured in Chrome 157: scrolled 300 px past the end of its own row, it was still at the top of the window). Give the screen a wrapper that ends where the staying should end. [ours: measured 2026-11-18, sample page 2026-11-17]
- An entrance inside a screen that stays plays when most of the element is in the window: `seen()` knows the element may sit low on that screen for as long as it holds, below the line the others cross. [ours: K web/kit/scroll.js, web/test_kit.mjs]

### When the script never arrives

A layout that only makes sense with the script running (a tall section with a sticky screen, entrances waiting to play) must fall back to the plain page when the script is blocked or fails, not only when JavaScript is off. Put this one line in the page's `<head>`, before the stylesheet, and write every such rule under `html.js`:

```html
<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js'); setTimeout(function () { if (!window.pageIsLive) { document.documentElement.classList.remove('js'); window.pageIsPlain = true; } }, 4000);</script>
```

With JavaScript off the class is never added; when the page's script fails to load or stops on an error, the class is taken away after four seconds and the visitor gets the plain page with its stills. The line is inline because it must run before the first paint, and it asks nothing of another host. Three things make it work: [ours: sample pages 2026-11-17, K web/kit, web/test_kit.mjs]

- The page's own script sets `window.pageIsLive = true` from its light part, as soon as the page is mounted and before anything heavy is asked for. A scene's library is then loaded on its own (`const THREE = await import('./vendor/three/three.module.min.js')`), so the four seconds guard against a script that failed, not against a slow connection: on a slow phone the page is alive in time and the scene joins it when its library arrives, its still standing in until then.
- A script that arrives after the four seconds stands down. The visitor is already reading the plain page, and a scene mounted over it, or entrances hidden again, would pull the page from under them. `stage.js` and `scroll.js` do this by themselves when `window.pageIsPlain` is set (no scene is mounted, `seen()` finishes everything, `count()` leaves numbers alone); the page's own code checks it first too: `if (window.pageIsPlain) return;` at the top of what it mounts.
- The stills that the plain page shows are in the markup, hidden by a rule under `html.js` (section 11), never inside a `<noscript>`.

### What still holds

- Everything above this section. The words are in HTML and complete without JavaScript; every moment has its still; under reduced motion nothing moves by itself, entrances are simply there, and only what the visitor's own scroll or choice changes may change. [ours: K check.mjs]
- One WebGL scene for the page, not one for each section. [judgement]
- The phone is composed, not squeezed: plan each moment again for a tall narrow screen and a thumb (the object above and the words below; a pointer moment becomes a scroll or a drag moment). Room left bare under a band for several screens is room the moment did not use. [judgement; ours: sample page 2026-11-17]
- Before showing it, open the `more-scroll-*.png` pictures (`--more` walks the page and pictures it screen by screen: the only pictures that show a fixed scene or a moment tied to the scroll) and answer four questions. Is each screen composed, or only filled? Would a crop of any of them be recognised as this brand? What happens when the hand stops? What does a visitor on reduced motion get (`more-reduced-*.png`)? [ours: K check.mjs]
- Have it read blind before it is shown: a reader who has seen neither the plan nor the code, given only the pictures and the check's numbers, says in one sentence what the page's idea is and lists what is weak, screen by screen (the site skill, step 4). Whoever built a page judges what they meant to build. On one sample page that reader found a closing hidden on the phone, plain patches cut out of a patterned ground, and one layout repeated four times, all of which the builder's own review had passed. [adapted: jury skill; ours: sample pages 2026-11-17]
- How smoothly it runs on a real phone cannot be checked here: say so when handing over. [ours: K check.mjs]
