### Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm, practical consequences
Source: W3C Internationalization, "Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm basics", https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/uba-basics (read 2026-11-17).
- Digits run left to right even inside right-to-left text; adjacent currency symbols attach to them.
- Spaces and punctuation are neutral. Between runs of opposite direction they take the base direction, so a trailing "!" or similar mark can land on the wrong side of mixed text.
- Fix: set `dir` on the containing element so the base direction matches the content, and isolate adjacent opposite-direction runs.

## 7. Motion

### Twelve principles of animation
Origin: Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, "Disney Animation: The Illusion of Life" (1981). The list was confirmed only through the Wikipedia article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation), a secondary source; the book itself was not read. One line each, in our wording.

1. Squash and stretch: shape changes give weight and flexibility, volume stays constant.
2. Anticipation: a small preparatory move before the main action.
3. Staging: present an idea so it is unmistakably clear and the eye goes to what matters.
4. Straight ahead and pose to pose: two ways of building animation, frame by frame or from key poses.
5. Follow through and overlapping action: parts stop or move at different times.
6. Slow in and slow out: more frames near the start and end of a move.
7. Arcs: natural movement follows curved paths.
8. Secondary action: supporting movement that enriches the main action.
9. Timing: number of frames sets speed, weight and mood.
10. Exaggeration: push the action to make it read.
11. Solid drawing: consistent volume and form in space.
12. Appeal: the character or object is engaging to look at.
(Read on 2026-11-17. Wikipedia's own table lists eleven headings; the twelfth, Appeal, is in other sections of the sources.)
