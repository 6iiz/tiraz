# Hooks: the first 1.5 seconds

Read this before writing the lines of any reel. Source tags: `[ours: …]` is a lesson or measurement we recorded (keys in `SOURCES.md`); `[adapted: …]` is a rule taken from a permissively licensed guide (notices in `THIRD-PARTY.md`); `[source: …]` is a public reference (`references.md`); `[judgement]` is reasoning without a recorded test. When an adapted rule and one of our tested lessons disagree, ours wins and the text says so.

The customer's `voice.md` decides the dialect, the tone and the banned words. The examples here show the mechanism in four dialects; copy the mechanism, never the sentence.

## 1. Why the hook gets this much care

- In the account insights behind these guides, the three reels measured each lost about half their viewers between second 2 and second 6, and only 3–8% reached the end. The one reel with a 35.5% skip rate got about 7 times the views of the two with 64–69%. [ours: N batch 9, insights 2026-11-14] This shows where viewers leave; it does not prove which hook pattern caused the difference.
- So the hook is not a title. It is everything the viewer receives before they decide: what is said, what is written, and what moves.

## 2. What a hook is made of (three layers, all inside 1.5 s)

| Layer | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shown | Frame 0 already has the subject on screen. Never a black, empty or logo-only first frame. | The kit fails a first frame that is nearly empty (luma std-dev under 6) [ours: K reel_check]; a reference that opened on 0.4 s of near-black was weaker for it [ours: N batch 9]. |
| Written | The on-screen hook line is visible by 1.0 s, 3–6 words, one accent word. | The check fails a reel whose first frame shows only the ground [ours: K reel_check]; some viewers watch muted (no confirmed share), so the line must work without sound [ours: P brief "read with sound off"]. |
| Said | If there is a voice, the first word is at 0.0–0.3 s, and it is the topic, not a greeting. | Voice from the first frame was approved after slower openings were called slow [ours: L 2026-11-16]; a reference whose first two seconds were a greeting spent the exact window where viewers leave [ours: N batch 10]. |
| Moving | Something moves with intent in the first 0.5 s (a counter runs, a card arrives, a line draws). | The strongest references all have motion or a live number at frame 0 [ours: N batch 9, batch 10]. |

- What the platforms themselves say: TikTok's ad guide tells advertisers to grab attention within the first three seconds and to skip long intros and logo animations [source: TikTok Video Advertising Guide]; Meta's Reels ads play with sound on by default unless the viewer muted [source: Meta Reels ads page]. No peer-reviewed study was found that measures how many viewers leave in the first 1–3 seconds [source: references.md §8], so the strongest evidence we have is the account data above. Do not quote a "muted viewing" percentage: none was confirmed on a primary page.
- Instagram says it can leave clickbait out of recommendations and recommends the original when it finds identical content [source: Instagram for Creators, recommendations and originality]. Two more reasons to pay off every promise and to copy mechanisms, never someone's reel.
- Write the on-screen line and the spoken line **separately**. The screen line is read before the voice is heard, so it is shorter and carries the hook alone. [adapted: instagram-agent-skill]
- **All layers point at the same idea, and the text sharpens the voice instead of repeating it.** If the voice says (Gulf) «قعدت شهر كامل أدوّر على الحل», the screen says «شهر… والحل زر واحد». A misaligned hook (clever line over a flat or unrelated picture) reads as confused. [adapted: viral-hooks (Vyral)]
- **Two cover tests:** cover the text and ask whether the picture alone, muted, makes you pause; then cover the picture and ask whether the text alone does. Both must pass. [adapted: viral-hooks (Vyral)]
- **Sound is the fourth layer and the timing cue:** the first sound lands on the first visible action (a tick on the counter, a click on the card), or a deliberate silence does. See `sound.md`. [adapted: viral-hooks (Vyral); ours: P18 "a sound for every action"]
- The cover text is usually the on-screen hook line, so it must also make sense as a still. [judgement]

## 3. Mechanisms that worked

Each mechanism below was seen in more than one strong reference or in our own work. Likes on references are weak evidence (comment-bait inflates them) [ours: P note after #15, N batch 6]; use these as patterns, not guarantees.

### 3.1 The hook is a task for the viewer
The viewer is asked to do something now: find the difference, guess, choose, listen. They are working, not watching, and they only get the answer by staying.
- Why it works: the answer is withheld; the reward is the viewer's own discovery, so it needs no claim at all. [ours: N batch 5 (the highest-liked reference in the library), N batch 6 "find a cut", N batch 6 "choose A/B/C/D before the reveal"]
- Make failure safe: if the viewer will fail the task, say it is normal, so curiosity grows without embarrassment. [ours: N batch 5]
- Give the key once, then let them try again. End where you began, so a replay repeats the experience. [ours: N batch 5]
- Kit: two items side by side on the brand ground, a `click` or `pop` on the reveal, `revealWords` for the answer.

### 3.2 A real number, ideally one that moves
A specific number from `facts.md`, and best of all a counter that runs from frame 0 and ends on the true value.
- Why: a moving number is proof and motion at once; specificity reads as knowledge. [ours: P12, confirmed in N batch 7 (a counter from frame 0 that matched the live API value)]
- A counter that stops at zero can be the shock itself. [ours: N batch 6]
- The number comes from `facts.md`, read today if it lives on a web page. A count that changes hourly (a stock price) gets no counter. [ours: K SKILL; A qa 2026-11-12]

### 3.3 A contradiction in two beats
State the expected half, then break it: «X… و Y». The gap between what the viewer believes and the line is the pull.
- We have used this shape on several covers, and their results are not measured yet: treat it as a pattern that reads well, not as a tested winner. [ours: T reels 19–24]
- The second beat must be paid off inside the reel (section 6).
- Stated form: name the common belief, then your claim against it. Implied form: state only the claim, and let the belief be understood. The bigger the believable distance, the stronger the pull. [adapted: content-skills viral-hooks]

### 3.4 Proof before the claim
Show live evidence of the pain or the benefit in the first 0.5 s, then say what it is.
- A search box ticking "0 results" while the question is typed; a result running before its name appears. [ours: N batch 9 (candidate pattern, one strong reference), N batch 10]
- Each named thing gets its visual proof within 1.2 s of being said. [ours: N batch 10]

### 3.5 Thousands, and only one
Many identical items in a muted colour; one in the accent colour, framed. The eye goes there before any word.
- Why: one accent is the whole sentence; it costs no reading time. [ours: N batch 4 (brand reference)]
- In the brand's colours: a quiet tone for the many, the accent for the one; one `ding` or `click` when the one is marked.

### 3.6 The viewer's pain, now
Name a situation the viewer is in today, in second person. «Your phone is full» beats «I found a trick».
- Why: a hook framed around the maker ("I did…") lets the viewer decide it is not about them. Swap "I/my" for "you/your"; a story told in first person still needs the viewer's own stake inside its first two lines. [adapted: content-skills viral-hooks]

### 3.7 Suspense with a promised event
Something is about to happen (a countdown, a cursor approaching a dangerous link), and it only happens at the end.
- Why: the promised event holds attention; the late payoff keeps them to the last second. [ours: N batch 4]
- Only when the event is honest. A fake build-up that never resolves is a broken promise.

### 3.8 A personal question with a real answer
«From which try did you get it?» works because everyone has an answer; «What do you think?» does not.
- A news hook that asks "what does this mean for you?" and answers "probably nothing" emptied its own promise: that reel saved 1 and shared 0, 65% under the account's 48-hour average. [ours: N "our reels in numbers", reel 17] One reel, so a warning, not a law.

## 4. Four ways a hook fails (diagnose, then fix)

[adapted: content-skills viral-hooks, applied to our cases]
1. **Late:** the topic arrives after 1.5 s (greeting, logo, "in this video"). Fix: delete everything before the first noun of the topic; often the hook is the third sentence of the draft.
2. **Confusing:** the line needs two reads (nested clause, abstract word, passive voice). Fix: one idea, ≤ 6 words on screen, plain words.
3. **Not for me:** clear but about the maker. Fix: second person, a pain the viewer already has.
4. **No gap:** clear and relevant, but nothing is left open. Fix: add the contrast, the task or the withheld answer.

Fast upgrades when a candidate is flat: cut its first three words; name the exact viewer (Levantine «لأي حدا عندو متجر أونلاين»); state what most people believe, then break it; put the one real number from `facts.md` in it. If two upgrades still leave it flat, the angle is wrong: pick another mechanism. [adapted: viral-hooks (Vyral)] Do **not** apply that guide's "make the number odd and specific" by rounding or inventing: the number is whatever `facts.md` says. [ours: K SKILL]

## 5. Scoring checklist (score every candidate)

One point per yes. A hook needs **8 of 10, and items 9 and 10 are mandatory**.

1. Frame 0 shows the subject (not empty, not only a logo).
2. Something moves with intent before 0.5 s.
3. The on-screen line is visible by 1.0 s.
4. The on-screen line is 6 words or fewer and has exactly one accent word.
5. The first spoken word (if voiced) is at ≤ 0.3 s, and the topic noun is in the first five words.
6. Picture, voice and text point at the same idea; the text sharpens the voice, it does not repeat it.
7. It is aimed at the viewer (second person or the viewer's situation).
8. It uses a mechanism from section 3 that was not used in the customer's last two reels. [ours: N idea bank rule]
9. **Every number, price and claim in it is in `facts.md`, word for word.** [ours: K SKILL]
10. **The reel pays it off: write the second at which the promise is delivered.** [ours: N reel 17]

A reel with no voice is scored on the same ten: item 5 counts as a yes, and item 6 is judged on picture and text alone. [judgement]

Also run the customer's `rules.md` "never" list on the line.

## 6. The promise and its payoff

- Write the payoff second next to the hook before writing anything else. If you cannot name it, the hook is not finished. [judgement]
- The payoff must be visible, not just said: the found difference highlighted, the counter landing, the fixed result on screen. The biggest sound hit goes there, not on the hook. [ours: P13]
- A picture is a claim too. An ending that shows a problem closed when the source says it is still open makes the reel false even if every line is true. [ours: A qa 2026-11-12]

## 7. Three genuinely different options

Always give the customer three hooks. They differ in **mechanism**, not wording.

- Each uses a different mechanism from section 3. If you need more shapes, the families used in short-form writing are: curiosity gap, contrarian, authority or proof, emotion, list or value stack, question, story, myth-buster, specificity or data, confession. Mix at least one that stops the scroll (task, number, contradiction) with one that holds (story, proof). [adapted: viral-hooks-skill (Roman Knox)]
- Each changes at least one of: the form (what is on screen), who speaks, where it happens. [ours: N idea bank rule]
- Vary the tone across reels too: dry irony, a whisper, mystery, a quiz host, a coach, a surprise; never the same hook style in two consecutive reels. [ours: S §12, customer correction]
- For each option give: mechanism · spoken line · on-screen line · what frame 0 shows · payoff second · score.

Bad (three rewrites of one idea):
«ليش جوالك بطيء؟» / «جوالك صاير بطيء؟» / «بطء الجوال له سبب»

Good (three mechanisms for the same reel, Gulf dialect, fact assumed to be in `facts.md`):
- Task: «وش الفرق بين هالجوالين؟» (two identical phones; one has the setting on)
- Proof first: a storage bar filling on frame 0, line «مو من الصور»
- Contradiction: «حذفت التطبيق… وملفاته للحين عندك»

## 8. The same mechanisms in four dialects

The bracketed values must come from `facts.md`. These are illustrations of shape; check every claim before use.

| Mechanism | Gulf | Levantine | Egyptian | Simple standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task | «وش الفرق بين هالرابطين؟» | «شو الفرق بين هالرابطين؟» | «إيه الفرق بين اللينكين دول؟» | «ما الفرق بين هذين الرابطين؟» |
| Number | «[العدد] طلب… وش يصير بعد ما تضغط اشترِ؟» | «[العدد] طلب… شو بيصير بعد ما تكبس اشتري؟» | «[العدد] أوردر… إيه اللي بيحصل بعد ما تدوس اشتري؟» | «[العدد] طلب… ماذا يحدث بعد الضغط على اشترِ؟» |
| Contradiction | «حذفت الصورة… وهي للحين عندهم» | «مسحت الصورة… وهي لسا عندهن» | «مسحت الصورة… وهي لسه عندهم» | «حذفتَ الصورة… وما زالت لديهم» |
| Pain, now | «جوالك ممتلي؟ مو من الصور» | «موبايلك مليان؟ مو من الصور» | «موبايلك مليان؟ مش من الصور» | «هاتفك ممتلئ؟ ليس بسبب الصور» |
| Personal question | «من أي مرة شفتها؟» | «من أي مرة شفتها؟» | «شفتها من أنهي مرة؟» | «من أي محاولة رأيتها؟» |

- Keep one dialect across voice, screen and caption. A Levantine voice with a Gulf caption reads as careless. [ours: D, principle]
- Words differ by dialect even for the same object (جوال / موبايل). Use the customer's word, from `voice.md`.

## 9. Never

- A promise the reel does not pay off, or pays off only in the caption. [ours: N reel 17]
- An invented, rounded-up or "about" number; a number not in `facts.md`. [ours: K SKILL, L reel 13 honesty]
- A greeting, a name, a logo sting or "watch till the end" before the topic. [ours: N batch 10]
- Selling a targeted, rare danger as a danger to everyone; fear must lead to a fix the viewer can do. [ours: A content-writer 2026-11-05]
- "Leaked", "secret" or "insider" framing without a source that says so. [ours: N batch 6]
- A big numbered title ("Top 5") as the hook; the number goes inside the line or in a small counter. [ours: P brief banned list]
- An imperative like «وقّف سكرول!» unless the payoff earns that urgency. [adapted: viral-hooks (Vyral)]
- A comment-bait line ("type X below") as the hook. Comment funnels without a visible promise did not lift comments (0.10 per like) and inflate numbers without saying anything about quality. [ours: P after #21, L 2026-11-12]
- The same mechanism twice in a row for the same customer. [ours: N idea bank rule]
